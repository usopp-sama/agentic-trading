## What this document is
The RBI's periodic report on **India's foreign-exchange reserves** - their size,
composition, and how they are managed. Reserves are India's external buffer and a
key input to RBI's rupee management. *Specific reserve figures are point-in-time;
read the latest from live data.*

## What reserves are made of
FX reserves comprise **foreign currency assets (FCA)** (mostly sovereign bonds
and deposits in reserve currencies), **gold**, **SDRs**, and India's **reserve
tranche position at the IMF**. They are managed with priority on **safety and
liquidity first, return second** - this is a buffer, not a return-seeking
portfolio.

## Why reserves matter to markets
Reserves provide **import cover** and the ammunition for the RBI to **smooth
rupee volatility** by intervening in FX markets. Ample reserves -> credibility,
ability to defend the rupee, lower external vulnerability, and support for the
sovereign rating. Falling reserves (especially via heavy intervention) signal
currency pressure.

## The intervention dynamic
When the rupee faces sharp depreciation pressure (capital outflows, oil spikes,
global risk-off), the RBI **sells dollars from reserves** to slow the move
(and buys to curb excessive appreciation / rebuild reserves). Reserve changes
therefore reveal the RBI's FX stance and the strength of external pressure.

## How an SME should use it
Treat **reserve adequacy (import cover, reserves vs short-term external debt) and
the direction of reserves** as a gauge of rupee stability and RBI firepower.
Rising/ample reserves = a more stable rupee and lower external risk (supportive
for INR assets); drawdowns amid outflows or an oil shock = currency-pressure
warning. Combine with the rate differential (IRP), oil prices and capital-flow
data when forming an INR view.
