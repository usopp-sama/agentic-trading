## The problem: official data is late
GDP and many macro aggregates are released with **long lags and big revisions**,
so by the time a recession or recovery is "official", markets have long since
moved. **Nowcasting** estimates the *current* state of the economy in real time
by blending many higher-frequency indicators before the official print lands.

## How nowcasting works
Combine timely indicators - PMIs, industrial production, electricity/freight,
auto sales, GST/tax collections, credit growth, trade, high-frequency mobility
and payments data - usually via a **dynamic factor model** that extracts a common
"state of activity" signal and updates it as each new data point arrives. The
output is a continuously revised estimate of growth and the probability of a
**turning point** (expansion <-> slowdown/contraction).

## Turning points are what matter
Markets are most sensitive at **inflection points** - when the cycle shifts from
expansion to slowdown (or vice versa). Catching the turn early is more valuable
than precisely measuring the level, because regime changes drive rotations across
sectors, rates and risk appetite.

## Caveats
Nowcasts are **noisy and get revised** as inputs update; false signals happen,
especially around one-off shocks. They indicate direction and probability, not
certainty, and should be triangulated across several independent indicators.

## How an SME should use it
Maintain a **real-time read of the cycle** from a basket of high-frequency
indicators rather than waiting for lagged GDP. Use the inferred regime to set
**cyclical vs defensive positioning**: early-expansion signals favour cyclicals
(financials, industrials, consumer discretionary, commodities); slowdown signals
favour defensives (staples, utilities, quality) and duration. Treat a turning-
point signal as a probabilistic regime alert to confirm with other evidence, not
a standalone trade trigger.
