## What this document is
An official **guide to reading the Union Budget** - it explains what each budget
document contains and defines the key terms. For an analyst it is the "map" that
makes budget-day numbers interpretable.

## The documents that make up the Budget
- **Annual Financial Statement** - the constitutional core (receipts/expenditure
  across Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund, Public Account).
- **Budget at a Glance** - the headline summary (receipts, expenditure, deficits,
  borrowing).
- **Finance Bill** - the tax-change proposals.
- **Expenditure & Receipt Budgets** - detailed spending and revenue.
- **Medium-Term Fiscal Policy / FRBM statements** - the deficit/debt trajectory.

## Key definitions an analyst must know
- **Revenue vs Capital:** revenue receipts/expenditure are recurring; **capital
  expenditure (capex)** builds assets and is the growth-positive line markets
  prize.
- **Fiscal deficit** = total expenditure - total receipts (excl. borrowings) =
  the government's borrowing need.
- **Revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure - revenue receipts (borrowing to fund
  consumption - lower quality).
- **Primary deficit** = fiscal deficit - interest payments (the "new" imbalance).

## Why the distinctions matter
The **composition** of the deficit matters as much as its size: a deficit driven
by **capex** supports growth and is bond-market-friendlier; one driven by the
**revenue deficit** is weaker. The **gross/net borrowing** the deficit implies
directly drives G-sec supply and yields.

## How an SME should use it
Use these definitions to read the Budget correctly on the day: focus on the
**fiscal deficit vs the FRBM path, the capex allocation and growth, and the
borrowing programme**. High-quality (capex-led) consolidation is positive for
cyclicals/infra and contains yields; revenue-led slippage and heavy borrowing
pressure bonds and the rupee. This is interpretive scaffolding - pair it with the
actual current-year Budget numbers.
