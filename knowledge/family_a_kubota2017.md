## The question: do US factors travel?
The paper tests Fama & French's five-factor model (market, size, value,
profitability, investment) on **Japanese equities**. It is a caution against
assuming a model fitted on US data is automatically the right description of a
different market - directly relevant to applying these factors in India.

## Key finding: not all factors replicate cleanly
In Japan the **value (HML) premium is robust**, but the newly added
**profitability (RMW) and investment (CMA) factors are weak or insignificant**.
The authors argue the five-factor model is **not clearly the best** model for
Japan; a value-augmented model can do as well or better. Factor reliability is
market-specific.

## Why this matters
Factor premia depend on market structure, accounting conventions, investor
base and sample period. A factor that is strong in the US can be muted
elsewhere. Blindly importing US factor weights risks tilting toward exposures
that are not actually compensated in the local market.

## How an SME should use it
Do not assume US-calibrated factor loadings hold in Indian equities. Treat
**value and momentum as the most globally robust** tilts; treat profitability
and investment as **useful-but-verify** signals whose local payoff should be
checked on domestic data before sizing up. When evidence is thin or conflicting,
prefer the factors with the strongest cross-country track record and demand
local confirmation before betting heavily on a factor that is mainly a US
finding.
