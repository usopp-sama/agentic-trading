## Buy outstanding growth businesses, hold for the long run
Fisher is the foundational **quality-growth** investor: the biggest gains come
from finding a few exceptional, well-managed companies with long runways and
holding them for years, not from trading in and out. The cost of selling a great
compounder early usually dwarfs the cost of riding volatility.

## The "scuttlebutt" method
Go beyond financial statements: talk to customers, suppliers, competitors,
ex-employees and industry experts to build a mosaic of a company's real
competitive standing. Qualitative, primary-source research uncovers durable
advantages and red flags that the reported numbers lag.

## The Fifteen Points (what makes a great business)
Fisher's checklist screens for durable growth, including:
- Products/services with a **long runway** of sales growth.
- Management committed to **developing new products** as old lines mature.
- Strong **R&D and sales organisation** effectiveness.
- **Above-average margins** and a plan to maintain/improve them.
- Good **labour, executive and cost/accounting** practices.
- Long-term **integrity of management** (treats minority holders honestly).
The thrust: superior, honest management running a business with a structural
growth edge.

## Conservative investors sleep well
A truly understood, high-quality business held with conviction lets you ignore
short-term price noise. Most "reasons to sell" (market level, recent price moves,
macro fear) are bad reasons; valid sells are a genuine deterioration of the
business or a clearly better opportunity.

## How an SME should use it
For a fundamental long thesis, weight **business quality and management
integrity/competence** heavily, not just cheapness. Favour firms with a long
growth runway, real R&D/innovation, defensible margins and shareholder-aligned
management. Use qualitative evidence (the scuttlebutt mosaic from news,
disclosures, channel checks) to confirm a durable edge, and let winners run -
sell on broken fundamentals, not on price wobbles.
