## Active return vs active risk
The whole discipline is about **active return** (return vs a benchmark, "alpha")
per unit of **active risk** (tracking error). The objective is to maximise
risk-adjusted active return, not raw return - measured by the **information
ratio (IR) = active return / active risk**. A good process is judged by its IR,
not by occasional big wins.

## The Fundamental Law of Active Management
**IR approx = IC x sqrt(breadth)**, where:
- **IC (information coefficient):** the skill/correlation between your forecasts
  and realised outcomes (usually small, e.g., 0.05).
- **Breadth:** the number of **independent** bets per year.
The lesson: you win either by being more skilled *or* by applying a modest skill
across **many independent opportunities**. Diversifying independent bets is how
small edges become a strong IR.

## Forecasting: refine raw signals into alphas
Raw signals must be turned into proper expected returns: **alpha = volatility x
IC x score** (the "refined forecast"). Shrink for reliability, neutralise
unintended exposures, and remember most of any signal is noise - so temper
forecasts toward the mean rather than taking them at face value.

## Implementation: optimise, and respect costs
Build portfolios by **mean-variance optimisation** of alpha against active risk
and constraints, then subtract **transaction costs** - which are real alpha.
High turnover can quietly consume the entire forecasted edge. The right trade
balances expected alpha against the cost and risk of getting there.

## How an SME should use it
Frame every active view as a **bet sized by conviction (alpha) and scaled by
risk**, and value a process by its **information ratio**, not isolated calls.
Prefer **many independent, modest-edge bets** over a few concentrated ones
(breadth), shrink noisy forecasts, neutralise unintended factor/sector tilts,
and always net out **transaction costs** before claiming an edge. Skill applied
broadly and cheaply is the durable source of active performance.
