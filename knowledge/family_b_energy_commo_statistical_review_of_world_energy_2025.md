## What this document is
The **Statistical Review of World Energy** is the authoritative annual dataset on
**global energy production and consumption** across oil, natural gas, coal,
nuclear, hydro and renewables, plus emissions. It is a structural/strategic
reference on the **energy mix and the energy transition**, not a price feed.
*Specific annual figures are point-in-time.*

## What it covers
Production, consumption and trade by fuel and by region, including the rise of
**renewables (solar/wind) and electrification**, the persistence of fossil fuels,
and the shifting balance between developed and emerging-market energy demand
(with Asia, India and China central to demand growth).

## The structural energy-transition story
The durable themes: **renewables are the fastest-growing source** and capturing a
rising share of power generation, **electricity demand is growing** (EVs, AI/data
centres, industrialisation), yet **fossil fuels still dominate** the absolute mix
and energy security remains a live constraint. The transition is real but
gradual and uneven.

## Why it matters for India
India is a **major, fast-growing energy demander** with heavy import dependence
(crude, LNG, coal) and an aggressive **renewables build-out** target. This frames
multi-year investment themes: **renewable power, grid/transmission, storage, EVs,
and energy security**, against continued reliance on coal and imported oil/gas.

## How an SME should use it
Use this as the **multi-year backdrop for energy and commodity sectors**:
structural tailwinds for renewables, power/grid, EV-chain and energy-transition
capex; persistent relevance of conventional energy (oil, gas, coal) for security
and baseload. It informs sector allocation and thematic conviction (e.g., Indian
power/renewables capex cycle), not short-term timing - pair with live commodity
prices and policy news for entries.
