## What the FRBM Act is
India's foundational **fiscal-discipline law**. It legally commits the central
government to limit deficits and debt, ensure inter-generational equity, and
bring **transparency and medium-term planning** to public finances. It is the
rulebook against which every Union Budget's fiscal stance is judged.

## The core commitments
- Reduce and contain the **fiscal deficit** (total borrowing) and **revenue
  deficit** (borrowing to fund day-to-day spending - the worse kind) on a glide
  path.
- Cap government **debt/guarantees** and improve debt sustainability over the
  medium term.
- The long-standing reference anchor is a **fiscal deficit around 3% of GDP**
  (with debt targets added by later amendments).

## Transparency and accountability
The Act mandates the budget to table **medium-term fiscal statements** (the
fiscal policy strategy, macroeconomic framework, and medium-term expenditure
framework), forcing the government to publish its deficit/debt trajectory and
explain deviations - which is exactly what markets scrutinise on budget day.

## Escape clause and flexibility
FRBM allows **temporary deviation** from targets in defined circumstances
(severe stress - e.g., national calamity, sharp downturn, structural reform).
This "escape clause" is why deficits can legitimately widen in crises (e.g.,
COVID), with a commitment to return to the consolidation path afterward.

## How an SME should use it
Judge each Budget's **fiscal deficit vs the FRBM glide path** and the
**quality** of the deficit (capex-led is growth-positive and bond-friendlier;
revenue-deficit-led is weaker). A credible consolidation path supports the
rupee, contains G-sec yields and aids rate-sensitive sectors; **fiscal slippage
or invoking the escape clause** pressures yields and the currency. Watch the
borrowing programme (gross/net G-sec issuance) the deficit implies, since that
drives the bond market.
