## Value comes from cash flows, growth and risk
Every asset's intrinsic value is the present value of its expected future cash
flows, discounted for risk. The three levers are: **how much cash** the business
generates, **how fast** that cash grows, and **how risky** (uncertain) it is.
Price is what you pay; value is what you get - investing edge comes from the gap.

## Two ways to value: intrinsic vs relative
- **Intrinsic (DCF):** project free cash flows, discount at a cost of capital,
  add terminal value. Forces explicit assumptions about growth and reinvestment.
- **Relative (multiples):** price a company off comparable firms (P/E, EV/EBITDA,
  P/B, EV/Sales). Faster and market-anchored, but inherits the market's mispricing
  and demands truly comparable peers.
Use both; when they disagree, understand *why*.

## Growth is not free - it must be funded
Growth only creates value when returns on new investment exceed the cost of
capital. Higher growth requires **reinvestment** (capex, working capital,
acquisitions), which consumes cash now. A "high-growth" story with poor returns
on capital can destroy value. Always tie growth to reinvestment and to ROIC.

## Match the story to the life cycle
Young/growth firms: value driven by total addressable market, scaling and
margins (high uncertainty). Mature firms: driven by efficiency, cash return and
capital discipline. Declining firms: by divestiture and cash extraction. Use the
narrative that fits the firm's stage, and keep narrative and numbers consistent.

## Risk and the discount rate
Riskier cash flows deserve a higher discount rate (cost of equity rises with
beta / market risk; cost of debt with default risk). Small changes in the
discount rate and terminal growth swing the answer a lot - so **stress-test**
the key inputs rather than trusting a single point estimate.

## How an SME should use it
Anchor a fundamental view on **intrinsic value with explicit, defensible
assumptions** (growth, margins, reinvestment, discount rate), then cross-check
with clean relative multiples. Demand a **margin of safety** - only act when
price is meaningfully below value. Treat valuation as a range, not a number;
flag the one or two assumptions the thesis is most sensitive to, and revisit
when the story (not the daily price) changes.
