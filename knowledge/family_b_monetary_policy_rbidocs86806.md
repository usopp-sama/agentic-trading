## What this document is
The RBI's official **Communication Policy** - how, when and through what channels
the central bank communicates. It matters to markets because **central-bank
communication is itself a policy tool**: clear, predictable communication
anchors expectations and reduces volatility; ambiguity creates it.

## Guiding principles
RBI communication is built on **relevance, transparency, clarity,
comprehensiveness and timeliness**, with the explicit aim of **anchoring the
expectations of economic agents** to enhance monetary-policy efficacy and
**minimise undue speculation**. Communication is tailored to different audiences
(markets, media, regulated entities, the public).

## Predictability is deliberate
The RBI pre-announces the **dates of structured communication via advance
release calendars** (policy statements, MPR, bulletins, committee reports).
Knowing *when* official signals arrive is part of how markets are kept orderly -
scheduled events (MPC decision, MPR) are the high-information moments.

## Channels and crisis communication
Signals flow through policy statements, the Governor's speeches/press
conferences, the MPR, bulletins, and increasingly social/audio-visual media,
with near-real-time market information on the website. The policy also covers
**communication during crises** and active monitoring to **counter
misinformation / fake news** about the RBI and regulated entities.

## How an SME should use it
Treat **scheduled RBI communications as the primary signal windows** - the MPC
statement, the Governor's tone, and the MPR carry the most information; weigh the
*language and stance shifts*, not just the rate number. Anchor interpretation to
official RBI channels and the release calendar, discount unscheduled rumours, and
remember the RBI is explicitly trying to anchor expectations - so a change in
*wording* (e.g., stance, "durable" inflation, liquidity guidance) is often the
real market-moving event.
