## What this document is
The RBI's annual **Report on Currency and Finance (RCF)** is a themed deep-dive;
the **2023-24 edition's theme is "India's Digital Revolution"** - how
digitalisation is reshaping the economy and the financial system. It is a
structural/strategic view, not a market data release.

## The digital public infrastructure (DPI) story
India's edge is its **digital public infrastructure**: Aadhaar (biometric ID),
**UPI** (real-time retail payments, a global leader by volume), and the broader
"India Stack". This DPI lowers transaction and remittance costs, widens financial
access, and improves the targeting of **direct benefit transfers** - a structural
productivity and formalisation tailwind.

## Finance is being reshaped
New players (FinTechs, BigTechs) and frontier tech (APIs, AI/ML, cloud) are
changing lending and payments: account aggregators, ONDC, frictionless-credit
platforms, and the **e-rupee (CBDC)** pilots. Digital lending enables instant,
data-driven credit and embedded finance - expanding the addressable market for
financial-sector firms.

## The risks the RBI flags
Digitalisation brings **cybersecurity, data privacy, third-party/vendor risk,
increased interconnectedness (systemic risk), fraud and mis-selling (dark
patterns)**. The central policy challenge is balancing **innovation against
financial stability and customer protection**, which drives evolving regulation
(digital-lending norms, SROs, sandboxes).

## How an SME should use it
Use this as the **structural thesis for India's financial and digital economy**:
a durable tailwind for payments, digital lending, fintech-enabled banks/NBFCs,
and formalisation-linked consumption. Weigh it against **regulatory risk** - RBI
is actively tightening rules on digital lending, data and interconnection, which
can hit specific business models. Treat it as a multi-year sectoral backdrop for
financials/fintech exposure, not a timing signal.
