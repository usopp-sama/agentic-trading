## A century of trend-following
A simple time-series-momentum strategy applied across equities, bonds,
commodities and currencies delivered **positive, consistent returns over ~110
years (1880-2010s)**, through very different macro regimes. The point: trend
following is not a recent data-mined artifact - it is remarkably durable
out-of-sample and across eras.

## The strategy tested
Combine 1-, 3- and 12-month trend signals per market, go long up-trends / short
down-trends, **size by volatility**, and aggregate into a diversified portfolio.
Returns were strong before costs and still solid after realistic costs, with a
Sharpe ratio meaningfully above 0 in nearly every decade.

## "Crisis alpha" - the key behavioural property
Trend following has tended to perform **well during sustained equity bear
markets** (e.g., 1930s, 2000-02, 2008) because it can go short falling markets
and long rallying bonds. This positive performance precisely when equities fall
is the strategy's most valuable feature for a portfolio: it is a convex,
diversifying return stream.

## When it struggles
It loses in **sharp, short-lived reversals and directionless, choppy markets**
(whipsaws), and in long flat ranges where trends never establish. The pain is
the price paid for the crisis-alpha convexity.

## How an SME should use it
Lean on multi-horizon trend (short + medium + long) as a **portfolio
stabiliser**, not a standalone alpha: expect it to cushion drawdowns when risk
assets sell off persistently, and to give back small amounts in calm or whippy
markets. Diversify trend signals across horizons and markets, scale by
volatility, and value it most for its negative correlation to equities in
crises rather than its standalone Sharpe.
