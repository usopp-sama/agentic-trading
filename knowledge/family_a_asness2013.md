## Value and momentum are pervasive
Both the value and momentum premia show up consistently across eight markets and
asset classes - individual stocks in several countries, country equity indices,
government bonds, currencies, and commodities. Neither is a stock-only quirk;
both look like common, global return phenomena.

## Negative correlation, so combine them
Value and momentum are negatively correlated. A roughly 50/50 combination has a
materially higher Sharpe ratio than either alone, because each tends to do well
when the other struggles. The headline practical lesson: diversify across the
two factors rather than choosing one.

## Common global factor structure
Value strategies are correlated with value elsewhere, and momentum with momentum
elsewhere, more than passive market exposures are. This points to common global
value and momentum factors - a momentum signal in one market carries information
about momentum in others.

## Liquidity-risk link
Momentum loads positively and value negatively on funding/liquidity risk. That
is part of why the blend is smoother: liquidity shocks that hurt momentum tend to
help value, so the two hedge each other through the cycle.

## Measurement conventions
Value uses slow-moving signals (e.g., book-to-market, or price relative to a
multi-year-ago level); momentum uses the past 12-month return skipping the most
recent month. Consistent definitions across assets make the premia comparable.

## How an SME should use it
Do not pick value vs momentum - blend them. When the book tilts hard onto one,
expect a correlated drawdown and treat the other factor as the natural hedge. A
combined value+momentum view is more robust than either standalone stance.
