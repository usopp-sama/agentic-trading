## The five rules
1. **Do your homework** - understand the business before you buy.
2. **Find economic moats** - durable competitive advantages.
3. **Have a margin of safety** - demand a discount to fair value.
4. **Hold for the long haul** - let quality compound; minimise turnover/costs.
5. **Know when to sell** - on broken thesis, overvaluation, or a better idea.
A disciplined process beats reacting to price.

## Economic moats - the central idea
A moat is a structural advantage that lets a company **earn returns on capital
above its cost of capital for years**. The main moat sources:
- **Intangible assets** - brands, patents, regulatory licences.
- **Switching costs** - costly/painful for customers to leave.
- **Network effect** - the product gets more valuable as more use it.
- **Cost advantages** - scale, process or unique resources.
Beware fake moats: great products, large size, or hot trends without one of the
above are not durable. Test a moat by whether high returns on capital *persist*.

## Reading the financials for quality
Favour consistent **free cash flow**, high and stable **returns on equity /
invested capital**, healthy margins, sensible leverage, and clean accounting.
Quality shows up as durable, self-funded profitability - not one good year.
Watch for the warning signs (declining margins, rising debt, aggressive
revenue recognition, serial dilution).

## Valuation and selling
Use multiples and cash-flow-based fair value, but only buy with a **margin of
safety**; a wonderful company at a terrible price is a bad investment. Sell when
the thesis is broken, the moat erodes, the price far exceeds value, or capital
is better deployed elsewhere - not because the price dipped.

## How an SME should use it
For company analysis, lead with **moat identification** (which of the four
sources, and is it widening or eroding?) backed by **returns-on-capital
durability**, then apply a margin-of-safety discount to fair value. Rank a long
idea higher when a clear moat, clean financials and a discount all line up;
downgrade "cheap" names with no moat or deteriorating returns. Frame sells
around thesis/moat damage, not price noise.
