## What it did: modernised the fiscal rulebook
The **N. K. Singh Committee** reviewed the original FRBM Act and recommended
shifting the primary anchor from the deficit alone to **government debt**, with
the deficit as the operational target. This reframed Indian fiscal policy around
**debt sustainability**, not just an annual deficit number.

## The headline targets (durable framework)
- A **debt-to-GDP anchor** for the general government (the Committee proposed
  ~**60%** overall - roughly **40% centre + 20% states**).
- A **fiscal-deficit operational target** stepping down toward ~**2.5-3% of
  GDP** over a medium-term path.
- Emphasis on a credible, rules-based **glide path** rather than ad-hoc targets.

## A clearer escape clause
The Committee tightened the conditions for deviating from targets (defined shocks
- national security, calamity, sharp growth collapse, far-reaching structural
reform), with **specified, limited deviation** and a duty to return to the path.
This is the basis for the disciplined-flexibility model used since.

## Why debt as the anchor
Debt captures the **cumulative** burden and sustainability; a single year's
deficit can mislead. Anchoring on debt aligns India with how markets and rating
agencies actually assess sovereign risk, and ties annual deficits to a longer-run
sustainability constraint.

## How an SME should use it
Assess fiscal credibility on **two axes**: the deficit vs its glide path *and*
the **debt/GDP trajectory**. Improving debt dynamics support sovereign ratings,
the rupee and lower yields; a rising debt path or repeated escape-clause use is a
macro risk (yield/currency pressure, crowding-out). Use this as the lens for
interpreting Budget math and the government's medium-term fiscal statements.
