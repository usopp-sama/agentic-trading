## What this document is
The **Finance Minister's Budget Speech for FY2026-27** - the government's annual
statement of fiscal priorities, tax changes and spending direction. It is one of
the highest-impact scheduled events for Indian markets. *Specific allocations and
rates below are point-in-time; verify against the actual budget documents before
acting.*

## The recurring fiscal narrative
The stated thrust is continuity: **fiscal prudence (consolidation along the FRBM
path) alongside a strong push on public investment (capex)**, structural reforms,
domestic manufacturing/atmanirbharta, energy security, and support for
employment, agriculture and household purchasing power - framed around sustaining
~7% growth with moderate inflation.

## Tax direction (as flagged in the speech)
- **Direct tax:** simplification and certainty; e.g., MAT proposed to become a
  final tax with the rate reduced to **14%** (from 15%), with accumulated MAT
  credit preserved. Middle-class relief is a recurring theme.
- **Indirect tax / customs:** tariff-structure simplification, removal of stale
  exemptions, correction of **duty inversion**, and support for domestic
  manufacturing and export competitiveness.

## Why markets care
Budget day moves rates, the rupee and sectors via three levers: the **fiscal
deficit and borrowing programme** (bonds), the **capex allocation and its mix**
(infra, capital goods, defence, railways), and **sector-specific tax/customs
changes** (winners and losers by industry).

## How an SME should use it
Read the Budget for **(1) the deficit/borrowing vs the FRBM glide path, (2) the
size and quality of capex, and (3) sectoral tax/tariff changes**. Capex-led,
consolidation-consistent budgets favour cyclicals/infra/capital goods and contain
yields; slippage pressures bonds and the rupee. Map specific customs/tax changes
to affected sectors (manufacturing, MSMEs, importers/exporters). Treat the
*direction and themes* here as durable; confirm exact numbers from the live
budget documents.
