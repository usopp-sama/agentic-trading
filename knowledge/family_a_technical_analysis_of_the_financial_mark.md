## Premise of technical analysis
Price action discounts everything (fundamentals, expectations, sentiment are
already in the price), prices move in **trends**, and history rhymes because
market psychology repeats. TA is the study of price/volume to judge the balance
of supply and demand - a complement to, not a replacement for, fundamentals.
Treat signals as probabilistic, never certain.

## Trend is the master concept
A trend is a series of higher highs/higher lows (up) or lower highs/lower lows
(down); markets also spend much time **sideways (no trend)**. Classify trend by
horizon (major / intermediate / minor). The first rule: **trade with the trend**
- continuation is the base case until structure breaks.

## Support, resistance and role reversal
Support is a price floor where demand has historically appeared; resistance a
ceiling where supply appears. Once broken, **support becomes resistance and vice
versa** (role reversal). Round numbers and prior highs/lows are common levels.
Breaks are more meaningful on higher volume.

## Volume confirms price
Volume should expand in the direction of the trend. Rising prices on rising
volume confirm strength; a breakout on weak volume is suspect. **Divergence**
between price and volume (or momentum) warns the move may be exhausting.

## Moving averages and momentum tools
- **Moving averages** smooth price and define trend; crossovers (price/MA or
  fast/slow MA) are classic trend signals but lag.
- **MACD** captures MA convergence/divergence for trend + momentum.
- **RSI / stochastics** flag overbought/oversold and, more usefully,
  **divergences** against price.
Oscillators work best in ranges; trend tools work best in trends - match the
tool to the regime.

## Patterns (continuation vs reversal)
Reversal patterns (head-and-shoulders, double tops/bottoms) suggest a trend is
ending; continuation patterns (triangles, flags, pennants) suggest a pause
before resumption. A pattern is only valid once **confirmed by a break** (ideally
on volume); anticipating it is lower-probability.

## How an SME should use it
Use TA mainly for **timing, levels and risk control**, not as a thesis on its
own: identify the prevailing trend, enter on pullbacks/breakouts confirmed by
volume, place stops just beyond the support/resistance that would invalidate the
setup, and respect divergences as early warnings. Combine with the fundamental
and factor views - when price action confirms a fundamental case, conviction is
higher; when they conflict, size down. Never treat a single indicator as
decisive.
