## What the model adds
The classic three-factor model (market, size, value) leaves systematic return
patterns unexplained. Fama & French add two factors so the model captures more
of the cross-section of average stock returns:
- **RMW** (Robust Minus Weak): long high-profitability firms, short low.
- **CMA** (Conservative Minus Aggressive): long low-investment firms, short
  firms that invest/grow assets aggressively.
The full set is **market, size (SMB), value (HML), profitability (RMW),
investment (CMA)**.

## The economic intuition
Profitability and investment are not arbitrary - they fall out of valuation
logic. Holding price fixed, firms with **higher expected profits** should earn
higher returns, and firms that must **invest heavily** (consume cash) to grow
should earn lower returns. So "quality" (profitable, disciplined-investment
firms) is a compensated tilt, not just a style label.

## Value (HML) becomes partly redundant
Once profitability and investment are in the model, **HML adds little** in US
data - much of the value premium is absorbed by RMW and CMA. The practical
read: a cheap stock that is also unprofitable and capital-hungry is a weak
buy; cheapness pays mainly when paired with decent quality.

## What it still misses
The five-factor model struggles most with **small, unprofitable, aggressively
investing** firms (persistent negative alpha). It also says nothing about
**momentum** - that remains a separate, additive factor (see Jegadeesh-Titman,
Asness et al.).

## How an SME should use it
Score a name on five durable dimensions, not just cheapness: market exposure,
size, value, **profitability (ROE/gross profitability)**, and **investment
discipline (asset growth, capex intensity)**. Prefer cheap-AND-profitable-AND-
disciplined names; be wary of cheap-but-junky small caps that screen as "value"
but are exactly where the model predicts disappointment. Treat these as
slow-moving, fundamental tilts and combine with a momentum overlay for timing.
