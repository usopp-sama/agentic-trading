## What this document is
The IEA's monthly **Oil Market Report (OMR)** - the benchmark assessment of
global oil **supply, demand, inventories and prices**. Oil is a first-order macro
variable for India (a large crude importer), so the OMR's balance view feeds
directly into inflation, the current account and the rupee. *Specific monthly
numbers are point-in-time.*

## What the OMR tracks (durable structure)
- **Demand:** global oil consumption growth, by region (OECD vs non-OECD, with
  China and India the key swing demanders).
- **Supply:** OPEC+ output and policy (cuts/unwinds), plus non-OPEC supply (US
  shale, Brazil, Guyana).
- **Balances & inventories:** the implied surplus/deficit and OECD stock levels -
  the core driver of the price direction.
- **Prices:** Brent/WTI and refining margins.

## Why oil matters for India
India imports the large majority of its crude, so the oil price is a major driver
of: **CPI/WPI inflation** (fuel + pass-through), the **trade/current-account
deficit**, the **rupee** (higher oil -> wider CAD -> weaker INR), and **fiscal
math** (fuel subsidies/excise). It is one of the cleanest macro transmission
channels in the Indian market.

## Sector transmission
- **Hurt by high oil:** OMCs (marketing margins squeezed if prices capped),
  aviation, paints/tyres/chemicals (crude-linked inputs), logistics.
- **Helped by high oil:** upstream producers (ONGC, Oil India) and some
  exporters/refiners on strong cracks.
The opposite holds when oil falls.

## How an SME should use it
Use the OMR's **supply-demand balance** to form an oil-price bias, then map it to
India: rising oil is a **macro headwind** (inflation up, CAD wider, rupee weaker,
rate-cut room reduced) and a sector rotation signal (favour upstream, fade
oil-sensitive consumers/aviation); falling oil is the reverse and broadly
INR-positive. Treat the report as the structural read on the oil cycle and pair
it with live Brent prices for timing.
