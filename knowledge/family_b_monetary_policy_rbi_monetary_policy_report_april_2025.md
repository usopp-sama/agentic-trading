## What this document is
The **Monetary Policy Report (MPR)** is the RBI's flagship half-yearly
publication (April and October) explaining the MPC's assessment of inflation,
growth and risks, and its forward guidance. It is the authoritative narrative
behind India's rate decisions. *Specific forecast numbers are point-in-time
(April 2025) and should be cross-checked against the latest live data, not
memorised.*

## How to read an MPR (the durable structure)
Each MPR works through the same blocks, which is what matters for analysis:
- **Macroeconomic outlook:** the MPC's CPI inflation and GDP growth projections
  (the key headline numbers and their balance of risks).
- **Prices and costs:** drivers of inflation - food, fuel, core, input costs.
- **Demand and output:** consumption, investment, external demand.
- **Financial markets and liquidity:** transmission of policy to money, bond,
  credit and FX markets.
- **External sector:** trade, capital flows, the rupee, reserves.

## The decision logic
The MPC sets the **repo rate** and a **stance** (accommodative / neutral /
withdrawal of accommodation) to steer CPI toward the **4% target** within the
**2-6% band** while supporting growth. Inflation projected above target ->
hawkish; comfortably within band with soft growth -> room to ease.

## What drives the surprise
Markets move on the **gap between the MPR's projections/stance and
expectations**, on revisions to the inflation/growth path, and on liquidity
signals (CRR, OMO, VRR/VRRR operations). Food-price and crude-oil assumptions
are the usual swing factors for the inflation path.

## How an SME should use it
Use the MPR for the **RBI's reaction function and the balance of risks**, not as
a source of current data. Map the projected CPI path and stance to the rate
cycle: a hawkish tilt is a headwind for bonds/duration and rate-sensitive
equities (banks, autos, real estate) and tends to support the rupee; a dovish
tilt is the reverse. Always pair the framework here with **live CPI, crude and
liquidity prints** before acting.
