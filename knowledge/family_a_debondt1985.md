## Long-term reversal: the overreaction hypothesis
Over 3-5 year horizons, prior "loser" portfolios (worst past 3-5 year returns)
go on to outperform prior "winners". Investors overreact to long runs of good or
bad news; prices later mean-revert as the overreaction unwinds. This is
long-term reversal (contrarian) investing.

## Formation and test windows
Rank stocks on ~3-5 years (e.g., 36 months) of past returns, form extreme
winner/loser portfolios, then measure returns over the following 3-5 years. Past
losers beat past winners by a large cumulative margin over the test window.

## Contrast with momentum (horizon matters)
This is the opposite sign to 3-12 month momentum. Short horizon (<=1 month):
reversal. Medium (3-12 months): momentum/continuation. Long (3-5 years):
reversal again. The holding horizon determines whether you fade or follow a
trend.

## Asymmetry and January seasonality
The rebound is larger for past losers than the give-back on past winners, and a
big share of the abnormal return clusters in January (tax-loss selling and
turn-of-year effects).

## Risks and caveats
Long-term losers are often distressed, small, illiquid names, so part of the
premium is compensation for risk, not pure mispricing. Long holding periods make
results sensitive to survivorship bias and delisting returns.

## How an SME should use it
Use as a multi-year valuation/contrarian overlay: deeply beaten-down names with
stabilizing fundamentals are reversion candidates, while multi-year darlings are
vulnerable. Do not apply at short horizons - there momentum dominates.
