## What the WTO is
The **World Trade Organization** sets and enforces the rules of global trade and
provides a forum for negotiation and **dispute settlement**. Its purpose is to
keep trade flowing predictably by lowering barriers and applying agreed rules -
the institutional backdrop against which tariffs, trade wars and export
policies play out.

## Core principles
- **Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN):** treat all trading partners equally - a
  concession to one generally extends to all.
- **National Treatment:** imported and domestic goods treated equally once in the
  market.
- **Bound tariffs:** members commit to ceiling tariff rates; predictability over
  protection.
- Exceptions exist (FTAs/customs unions, anti-dumping/countervailing duties,
  safeguards, development carve-outs).

## Tariffs and trade barriers - the mechanics
A **tariff** is a tax on imports that raises the price of foreign goods,
protecting domestic producers but raising input/consumer costs and inviting
**retaliation**. Non-tariff barriers (quotas, standards, licensing) do similar
work. Trade tensions (tariff hikes, retaliation, supply-chain reshoring) reshape
winners and losers across export- and import-exposed sectors.

## Why it matters for India
Trade policy drives **export competitiveness** (IT, pharma, textiles, autos,
chemicals, gems & jewellery), **import costs** (electronics, crude, edible oil,
capital goods), and **supply-chain shifts** (the "China+1" / friend-shoring
theme that can benefit Indian manufacturing). Tariff actions by major economies
(US/EU/China) ripple into Indian exporters and the rupee.

## How an SME should use it
Read trade/tariff news through **sector exposure**: tariff threats or barriers
on a product hurt the exposed Indian exporters (or help import-competing domestic
producers), while supply-chain reshoring (China+1) is a structural tailwind for
Indian manufacturing/PLI sectors. Watch **major-economy tariff moves and
retaliation** as catalysts for export-heavy sectors and the rupee, and use WTO
rules/disputes to gauge whether a trade action is likely to stick or be rolled
back.
