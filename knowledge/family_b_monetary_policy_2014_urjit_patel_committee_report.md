## What it established: India's inflation-targeting framework
This is the blueprint for modern Indian monetary policy. It recommended that the
RBI adopt **flexible inflation targeting (FIT)**, with **CPI (Consumer Price
Index) as the nominal anchor** - replacing the earlier multiple-indicator,
WPI-leaning approach. Its recommendations were institutionalised in the
2015-16 RBI Act amendment and the **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**.

## The key numbers (durable framework)
- **Target: 4% CPI inflation**, with a tolerance band of **+/- 2% (i.e., 2-6%)**.
- Policy is set by a **committee (MPC)**, not a single Governor - decisions by
  vote, with accountability if inflation stays outside the band.
- A glide path was prescribed to bring then-high CPI down to target over time.

## Why CPI and why a band
Households experience CPI (food + fuel + core), so anchoring expectations
requires targeting what people actually feel. The **band acknowledges supply
shocks** (food/fuel) the RBI cannot control short-term, giving "flexible"
room to support growth while keeping medium-term inflation anchored.

## Implications for markets
A credible inflation target makes **rate decisions more predictable**: the RBI
hikes when CPI runs hot toward/above 6% and eases when it falls toward/below 2%
with growth weak. This shapes the rate cycle that drives bond yields, the rupee,
and rate-sensitive equity sectors (banks/NBFCs, autos, real estate).

## How an SME should use it
Read RBI actions through the **4% +/- 2% CPI lens**: inflation near the upper
band implies a hawkish bias (rate hikes, tighter liquidity - headwind for
duration and rate-sensitives); inflation near the lower band with soft growth
implies easing (tailwind). Treat the **MPC's stance and CPI trajectory** as the
primary macro driver of Indian rates and the rupee, and judge surprises by how
far CPI sits from target rather than by the absolute rate level.
