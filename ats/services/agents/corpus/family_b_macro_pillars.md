# Macro pillars primer (Family B) — India focus

## Interest rates & monetary policy (RBI)
The RBI's repo rate sets the price of money. Rate hikes cool inflation but
slow growth and compress equity valuations (higher discount rate). Rate cuts
are generally bullish for rate-sensitive sectors: banks, autos, real estate,
NBFCs. Watch the MPC calendar, the policy stance (accommodative/neutral/
withdrawal), and the 10-year G-Sec yield as the market's read on the path.

## Inflation (CPI/WPI)
High CPI erodes real returns and pressures the RBI to tighten. Food and fuel
dominate Indian CPI and are volatile. Falling inflation gives the RBI room to
cut — usually risk-on. Persistent inflation hurts consumption names and fixed
margins.

## Currency (USD/INR)
A weakening rupee helps exporters (IT, pharma) by inflating foreign earnings in
rupee terms, and hurts importers and firms with dollar debt. Sharp INR moves
often track crude prices, the US dollar index (DXY), and foreign portfolio
flows (FII/FPI).

## Crude oil
India imports ~85% of its oil, so crude is a master macro variable. Rising
crude widens the current-account and fiscal deficits, pressures the rupee, lifts
inflation, and squeezes oil-marketing companies, paints, tyres, aviation, and
logistics. Falling crude is broadly positive for India.

## Fiscal policy & the Union Budget
Government capex drives infrastructure, capital goods, cement, and defence.
Deficit targets affect bond yields. The Budget (Feb 1) and GST collections are
key signposts.

## Global linkages
US Fed policy and Treasury yields drive global risk appetite and FII flows into
EM equities. China's growth affects metals and commodity demand. Geopolitical
shocks (wars, sanctions, shipping disruptions) hit oil and supply chains.

## Monsoon & rural demand
A good monsoon lifts rural incomes — positive for FMCG, two-wheelers, tractors,
and agri inputs. Drought does the reverse.

## How to translate macro into a tilt
Map each pillar to a directional pressure on the broad market or a sector, then
net them. State your confidence and the single biggest macro risk to the view.
A macro tilt should bias the book, not pick individual stocks.
