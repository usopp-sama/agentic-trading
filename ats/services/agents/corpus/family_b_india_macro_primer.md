# India Macro Primer (Family B)

Grounding for the macro / pillars-of-the-economy experts on the drivers that move
Indian equities at the index and sector level.

## Monetary policy (RBI)
- The Reserve Bank of India sets the repo rate; the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)
  meets roughly every two months.
- Rate cuts ease financial conditions and tend to support rate-sensitive sectors
  (banks, NBFCs, autos, real estate); hikes do the reverse.
- The RBI targets CPI inflation around 4% (+/- 2% band). Inflation surprises move
  bond yields and equity valuations.

## Growth and activity signals
- GDP growth, PMI (manufacturing and services), IIP (industrial production), and GST
  collections proxy the real economy.
- High-frequency reads: auto sales, credit growth, e-way bills, power demand.

## External sector and currency
- USD/INR matters: a weaker rupee helps exporters (IT, pharma) and hurts importers
  (oil, capital goods) and companies with dollar debt.
- Crude oil is a key import; rising crude widens the trade deficit and pressures
  inflation, the rupee, and oil-sensitive sectors.
- Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI/FII) flows are a major swing factor for large caps;
  domestic institutional (DII) flows often cushion FPI selling.

## Fiscal and policy
- The Union Budget (February) sets fiscal deficit targets, capex, and sector incentives
  (e.g., PLI schemes) that can re-rate sectors.
- Government capex on infrastructure supports capital goods, cement, and construction.

## Global linkages
- US Federal Reserve policy and US 10-year yields influence FPI risk appetite toward
  emerging markets including India.
- Global risk-off episodes typically hit high-beta Indian mid/small caps hardest.

## How to use this
- Translate a macro read into a market-wide tilt (risk-on vs risk-off) and a sector
  lean, not a single-stock call. Combine with bottom-up signals before sizing.
